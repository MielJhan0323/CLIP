## Why it worked (the 7 elements)
1. **Universal question, absurd answer.** You don't need to know football or Trevor. Everyone has a GOAT opinion (Jordan vs LeBron), so the clip reaches past the Jags audience. The answer is so wrong it's funny, so people comment, quote it and send it to friends ("bro said DELLY").
2. **A curiosity gap set in the first second.** The caption claims his GOAT is "better than LeBron" but doesn't say who. The only way to find out is to keep watching. The name is never shown before 10.3 s (the reel only lands on Delly at the reveal).
3. **The viewer plays along.** The head reel shows the obvious candidates while he thinks, so the viewer is guessing ("Jordan? Kobe?") and is invested in the answer. That turns dead air into the most tense part.
4. **Dynamic contrast.** It goes quiet, then hits hard: a 15+ dB jump from the thinking silence to the name hit. The quiet makes the reveal feel bigger.
5. **Payoff at about 2/3, then proof.** The reveal lands at 68 %, and the stats table gives a second laugh (0 MVPs, the box drawing round it). The viewer isn't left waiting after the reveal; they get a reason to stay to the very end.
6. **Short and loopable (15 s).** High completion rate. The last line ("I can feel the tension") is a reaction, not a wrap-up, so the loop back to the question feels natural, and rewatches count as views.
7. **A new beat every 1.5–2.5 s.** Card → paper flashes → whoosh → typed text + reel → strobe/name → table → box draw. There's never a stretch where nothing changes.

## How to harness it (do this on every future clip where it fits)
**Pick clips that have this shape:** *question everyone can answer → pause → unexpected / funny answer → reaction*. Good questions are GOATs, "who would you…", "dream X", rankings, "what would you pick", prices ("how much is this worth?"). The answer should surprise.

**Edit template (15–18 s):**
1. **0–2.5 s: question + provocative claim.** YouTube card or big question text; the top caption makes a bold claim *about* the answer without giving it away ("…is better than LeBron?", "…put JESUS in his foursome?").
2. **2.5–4 s: pattern interrupt on the key word.** Word lock / echo / whip. Keep it short.
3. **Thinking or setup beat: let the viewer guess.** Show candidates (head reel, options list, multiple-choice tiles, a "?" slot). Keep the audio quiet here (music low or out, only the soft reel ticks), so there's tension.
4. **Reveal at about 60–70 % of the runtime**, the loudest moment: strobe + ding (+ fahhh if it's a crazy answer). Captions and graphics never show the answer early.
5. **Proof / second laugh right after**: stats, comparison table, receipt, value. Plain broadcast look, with a box or ring that draws on.
6. **End on the reaction line** (≤1.5 s after the last laugh). No outro, so it loops.
7. **Title/description end on a question to the viewer** ("Who's your GOAT?"), which drives comments.

**Checklist before rendering:**
- [ ] Could a non-football fan understand the question in 1 s?
- [ ] Is the answer hidden until 60–70 %?
- [ ] Does the viewer have something to guess during the wait?
- [ ] Is there a quiet stretch before the reveal and a loud hit on it?
- [ ] Is there a proof/second-laugh beat after the reveal?
- [ ] Does it end within about 1.5 s of the last laugh and loop cleanly?
- [ ] ≤ 18 s?
